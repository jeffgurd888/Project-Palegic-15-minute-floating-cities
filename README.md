### White Paper: Project Pelagic
**Date:** August 2026 | **Status:** Conceptual Engineering Blueprint

#### 1. Executive Summary
This paper presents a paradigm shift in offshore infrastructure: a mobile, linear production system capable of extruding fully functional, tsunami-resistant floating islands directly at sea. The system utilizes 1 specialized pump truck, 3 continuous mixers, and interwoven rebar hinges.

#### 2. Problem Statement
Traditional floating structures require massive dry docks, heavy towage, and rigid steel molds. They are susceptible to fatigue and catastrophic failure under high-energy wave regimes.

#### 3. System Architecture & Material Science
- **Layer 1 (Bottom):** Self-expanding chemical foam cement (400–450 kg/m³).
- **Layer 2 (Middle):** Fiberglass-reinforced foam cement (800 kg/m³).
- **Layer 3 (Top):** Polymer/epoxy-infused rapid-set concrete (1,800 kg/m³).
- **Formwork:** Vacuum-formed HDPE/PVDF honeycomb grid that remains permanently embedded as sealed air chambers.

#### 4. Manufacturing Methodology ("Pizza Conveyor")
1. **3 Continuous Mixers** (for each layer).
2. **1 Peristaltic Pump Truck** (preserves foam bubbles, not piston type).
3. **Station A:** Robotic arm drops hinged rebar cages.
4. **Station B:** 3-in-1 nozzle pours all layers.
5. **Station C:** Roller embeds fiberglass mesh.
6. **40m Heat Tunnel:** Forced air + IR lamps at 60°C–80°C, reducing cure time to 45–60 minutes.

#### 5. Structural Continuity
As the cured panel exits the tunnel, the liquid cement of the *new* panel encapsulates the protruding hinge bars of the *previous* panel, creating a chemically fused, cold-joint-free slab.

#### 6. Tsunami Resilience (Air-Pressure Dome)
- Low aerodynamic profile allows water to flow over rather than crash against.
- Dome pressurized to 1.05–1.1 atm. Tsunami overtopping compresses internal air, acting as a pneumatic spring to absorb kinetic energy.

#### 7. Logistics
- 2-acre island requires 5–7 days of continuous production.
- Requires 3 full-time supply tug rotations per day.

#### 8. Risk Assessment
- **Exothermic Runaway:** Embedded thermocouples adjust conveyor speed to keep core below 90°C.
- **Validation:** Lab-scale adhesion tests, 10m prototype load tests, wave tank simulation for pneumatic shock absorption.

### 📦 Step 1: Raw Material Intake
Tug-and-hopper barges dock with dry cement, foaming agents, fibers, epoxy, HDPE plastic spools, and carbon-fiber dome panels.

### 🧪 Step 2: Continuous Mixing (3 Mixers)
- Mixer A: Foaming agent + lightweight cement (Bottom layer)
- Mixer B: Fiberglass + medium-density cement (Middle layer)
- Mixer C: Epoxy + rapid-set concrete (Top layer)

### 🚛 Step 3: Peristaltic Pump Truck
Specialized multi-channel **peristaltic (squeeze-tube) pump**. Standard piston pumps would crush the foam's air bubbles.

### 🔲 Step 4: Honeycomb Forming
HDPE/PVDF plastic is vacuum-formed into a honeycomb grid. Acts as mold during pour, remains embedded as permanent sealed air chambers.

### 🦾 Step 5: Rebar Integration
Robotic arm deposits interwoven hinged rebar cages with universal joints at edges.

### 🏗️ Step 6: 3-Layer Pour (3-in-1 Nozzle)
1. Bottom: Self-expanding foam (400 kg/m³)
2. Middle: Fiberglass spine (800 kg/m³)
3. Top: Epoxy-infused armor (1,800 kg/m³)
4. Roller embeds fiberglass mesh into top layer.

### 🔥 Step 7: The 40m "Pizza Oven" Tunnel
Heat tunnel at 60°C–80°C reduces curing time from 24 hours to 45–60 minutes.

### 🔗 Step 8: Monolithic Water Entry
New panel's liquid concrete fully encapsulates previous panel's protruding hinge bars. Creates a cold-joint-free slab.

### 🌊 Step 9: Dome Installation
- Vacuum-sealed groove on top.
- Carbon-fiber/polycarbonate panels bonded with epoxy.
- Pressurized to 1.05 atm to act as pneumatic spring against tsunami surges.
</details>

## Overview
This repository hosts the conceptual engineering blueprint for a mobile, linear production system capable of extruding fully functional, tsunami-resistant floating islands directly at sea. The system integrates vacuum-formed polymeric buoyancy cassettes, a three-layer self-expanding foam-cement composite, and a continuous heat-curing "pizza oven" conveyor.

## Key Innovations
- **3-Layer Composite Core:** Bottom flotation (400 kg/m³), middle spine (800 kg/m³), top armor (1,800 kg/m³).
- **Peristaltic Pump System:** Preserves microscopic air bubbles in the foam layer.
- **40m Heat Tunnel:** Reduces curing from 24 hours to 45–60 minutes.
- **Interwoven Hinged Rebar:** Creates a seamless, monolithic slab with zero weak joints.
- **Pneumatic Dome:** 1.05 atm pressurization absorbs tsunami kinetic energy via air compression.
